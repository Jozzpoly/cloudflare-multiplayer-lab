# Multiplayer Foundation — Program Control

Status: AGENT-OPERATIONAL / OWNER-LOCKED MISSION / ADAPTIVE ROADMAP
Updated: 2026-09-18

## Purpose

This document is the durable control plane for the active Multiplayer Foundation program.

It exists so a fresh or continuing Browser GPT can recover the real objective, select the next useful action without Owner micromanagement, and change tactics when evidence changes without silently changing the project mission.

The roadmap below is a capability tree and decision framework, not a ticket queue.

## Mission lock

Multi_World is a multiplayer/shared-world technology laboratory and donor source.

The active program is Multiplayer Foundation.

The accepted early target is a professional, reusable real-time physical multiplayer substrate for 1–6 dynamic actors, later qualified with real 3–6-human play.

The Shared Yard is a falsification crucible. Gameplay/content is allowed only when it is the minimum or materially better test surface for a concrete multiplayer question.

Only explicit Owner direction may change:
- repository role;
- long-horizon mission;
- active program;
- success objective.

Everything below that strategic boundary may be changed autonomously when evidence warrants it.

## Success model

The foundation is not successful because CI is green or because every planned experiment ran.

It should eventually leave recipient projects with defended contracts for:
- coherent shared physical consequence;
- responsive player agency;
- dynamic actor admission, departure and replacement;
- separation of WorldEpoch, roster, ActorSession, transport and device lifetimes;
- bounded recovery across realistic transport and runtime disruption;
- an explicit network impairment envelope rather than hidden assumptions;
- browser and mobile lifecycle behavior;
- measured fault/load limits and only evidence-earned scaling mechanisms;
- real 3–6-human evidence that another person feels present in the same physical place;
- donor-ready contracts, provenance and known nonclaims.

## Cross-cutting invariants

These survive tactical replanning unless the Owner changes the mission or evidence directly disproves the invariant itself.

1. Shared truth must remain authoritative and measurable. Do not buy apparent smoothness by weakening exact-state evidence silently.
2. Player agency matters independently from exactness. A world that stays exact while a player's commands no longer reach authority is not a successful multiplayer state.
3. World lifetime, roster lifetime, ActorSession lifetime, transport lifetime and device/page lifetime are distinct concepts.
4. Dynamic topology must not be simulated by hidden epoch resets unless the experiment explicitly targets that mechanism.
5. Negative evidence is retained. A later PASS does not erase an earlier RED; classify why they differ.
6. A test PASS proves only its stated contract and specimen.
7. Human feel is an Owner authority boundary; routine causal and machine validation is not.
8. New architecture must be earned by a concrete pressure. Do not add rollback, ownership, persistence, interest management, higher rates or larger scale by convention.
9. Mobile remains an early client pressure, not a late compatibility phase.
10. Repository mutation should remain bounded, reversible and attributable to an active multiplayer unknown.

## Capability tree

The tree expresses dependencies and pressure areas. It is intentionally not a fixed linear schedule.

### F1 — Dynamic shared-world composition

Target:
- 1–6 dynamic actors in one WorldEpoch;
- shared active physics;
- browser self + N;
- join/leave/replacement without false identity continuity;
- exact topology transition/rebase;
- useful player agency under the declared timing envelope.

Promotion condition:
The machine crucible can compose the required actor count and topology changes while preserving both exact shared truth and authority-realized control inside a declared bounded network envelope.

### F2 — Identity and lifecycle separation

Target:
- ActorSession independent from one socket/page;
- protected and replaceable capacity semantics;
- same-epoch roster mutation where justified;
- clean handling of stale, disconnected and returning actors;
- no accidental coupling of actor lifetime to transport liveness.

Promotion condition:
Lifecycle transitions are causally defended under active shared physics and do not require hidden world resets to remain coherent.

### F3 — Recovery and world continuity

Target:
- transport recovery;
- bounded exact rebase;
- runtime/process reconstruction where evidence earns it;
- durable checkpoint semantics that preserve semantic world truth;
- explicit distinction between recoverable interruption and genuinely retired world state.

Promotion condition:
Required failure classes can recover or fail closed without identity fabrication, silent state loss or misleading continuity claims.

### F4 — Timing, impairment and agency envelope

Target:
- latency/jitter behavior;
- ordered-stream queue pressure;
- temporary transport loss;
- late/missing canonical input behavior;
- prediction/authorship/reconciliation policies;
- explicit operating envelope and graceful behavior outside it.

Promotion condition:
Exactness, player agency and recovery behavior are characterized together. The foundation must not declare a network regime supported merely because state guards remain exact.

### F5 — Browser/mobile/platform lifecycle

Target:
- visible/hidden transitions;
- page refresh/re-entry;
- background scheduling pressure;
- mobile touch/control paths;
- browser process/device disruption that materially affects the multiplayer contract.

Promotion condition:
Representative platform lifecycle transitions preserve the claimed identities and shared-world behavior without requiring Owner workarounds.

### F6 — Fault/load scaling

Target only when pressure appears:
- CPU/memory/bandwidth pressure;
- 1–6 concurrent active bodies and props;
- fault combinations;
- locality and deployment topology;
- interest management only when measured pressure justifies it.

Promotion condition:
Measured limits and bottlenecks are known, and any scaling mechanism introduced solves a demonstrated problem rather than an imagined future one.

### F7 — Real 3–6-human qualification

Target:
- representative natural join/leave;
- simultaneous movement and shared physical interaction;
- latency from real devices/locations available to the Owner;
- recoveries that occur naturally during play;
- legibility of other humans as present physical actors.

This is not a substitute for earlier machine falsification. It is the human qualification layer after technical readiness.

### F8 — Donor closure

Target:
- compact integration contracts;
- exact qualified source/runtime anchors;
- known assumptions and nonclaims;
- reusable tests where they carry real value;
- recipient-oriented donor boundaries without forcing all future projects into this repository's current substrate.

This is earned only after the underlying foundation is useful enough to donate.

## Roadmap semantics

Each active node is classified as one of:
- OPEN — materially unresolved;
- VERIFYING — a discriminator is running or evidence is incomplete;
- EARNED — bounded contract qualified;
- DEFERRED — real question, lower information value now;
- REJECTED — hypothesis/architecture/path falsified;
- OWNER_BOUNDARY — next material information requires human judgement or strategic authorization.

Do not turn EARNED into universal truth. Preserve scope.

Do not work DEFERRED nodes merely because they exist.

When a node closes, ascend to the nearest parent objective and ask what unresolved child now has the highest decision value. Do not pivot sideways into a new program.

## Meaning of “continue” / “kontynuuj”

A short continuation command authorizes autonomous work inside the existing Owner-locked program and already established mutation envelope.

Interpret it as:

1. Recover mission and the active capability node.
2. Read the live-state pointer instead of relying on conversational recency alone.
3. Reverify volatile facts that can change the decision: branch HEAD, current CI/run, deployment/runtime/session when relevant.
4. Infer the current work mode: ORIENT, DISCOVER, VERIFY, DESIGN, BUILD, SUPERVISE or OWNER TEST.
5. State internally the nearest unresolved multiplayer unknown and at least the strongest competing explanation when ambiguity matters.
6. Choose the smallest faithful action with the best decision-relevant information per total cost.
7. Execute through the natural local boundary; do not stop merely because one command/test/commit completed.
8. Classify the result before changing architecture.
9. Update live state only when a material fact, decision, frontier or hypothesis changed.
10. If the active node is closed, ascend to its parent and continue from the next unresolved child.
11. Stop only at a genuine Owner boundary, consequential authorization boundary, missing access that cannot be recovered, or a low-information natural stop.

“Continue” never means:
- blindly execute the next bullet;
- create a new feature because the previous experiment passed;
- keep a campaign alive after its information value is exhausted;
- rewrite the mission from an agent hypothesis;
- turn every unresolved capability into mandatory work.

## Plan correction triggers

Re-plan immediately when any of the following becomes material:
- fresh evidence contradicts the working hypothesis;
- two supposedly equivalent specimens disagree;
- a gate is discovered to measure projection rather than the required causal property;
- a cheaper or more faithful evidence route becomes available;
- the active experiment stops discriminating between hypotheses;
- an Owner correction changes intent or priority;
- a local node closes and the next planned task no longer serves its parent;
- implementation complexity grows faster than information gained;
- a proposed action cannot be traced to an unresolved Foundation capability.

The correct response to one of these triggers is not “finish the old roadmap first”. Re-ground, re-rank and continue from current truth.

## Experiment contract

Before a material experiment or runtime change, be able to answer:
- What exact unknown is being tested?
- What are the strongest plausible competing outcomes?
- What observation would discriminate them?
- Which identities bind source, run, runtime and world/session evidence?
- What is required evidence versus corroboration?
- What would count as apparatus failure rather than mechanism failure?
- What change is intentionally excluded?
- What result changes the next decision?

Use negative controls when they materially improve discrimination.

Do not raise thresholds, delays or timeouts merely to manufacture PASS.

## Evidence hierarchy

Keep these layers distinct:
- source/build identity;
- authority/canonical state;
- client private/predicted state;
- application convergence;
- browser presentation;
- Owner session/device;
- human judgement.

A lower layer cannot impersonate a higher layer.

A rendered or predicted self motion does not by itself prove authority-realized player agency.

## Owner interaction contract

The agent should normally own:
- source/history recovery;
- research;
- implementation;
- CI;
- diagnostics;
- exact comparisons;
- bounded experimental design;
- failure classification;
- branch hygiene inside the active authorized program;
- documentation-state maintenance.

Ask the Owner when the missing information is genuinely:
- feel or perceptual quality;
- taste/interest;
- strategic priority between materially different project directions;
- physical device experience unavailable through faithful automation;
- high-consequence/destructive/external authorization;
- final acceptance of a human-facing experience.

Do not turn the Owner into a test runner or project scheduler.
